Chapter 6 covers the urinary system — the body’s blood filtration and fluid regulation system. Specialty: Urology (urologist). KEY: The suffix -uria (condition of urine) appears in nearly every clinical term in this chapter.
3 System functions
Filtration
Cleans the blood
Kidneys filter ~180 L of blood per day, producing ~1.5 L of urine. Every drop passes through the kidneys ~60 times daily. Removes urea, creatinine, excess salts, and water.
Regulation
Blood pressure
Fluid volume control + RAAS (renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system). Kidneys release renin → activates a BP-raising cascade. Renal hypertension = BP elevation from kidney disease.
Homeostasis
Maintaining balance
Fluid/electrolyte balance (Na, K, Cl, Ca), acid-base balance (pH 7.35-7.45), erythropoietin (RBC production), vitamin D activation.

A&P terms
TermDefinition
BladderMuscular hollow organ (cyst/o, vesic/o) storing urine. Pelvic cavity, capacity ~400-600 mL. Trigone (trigon/o) = triangular base where ureters enter and urethra exits — most common site of UTI and bladder cancer.
HilumConcave medial indentation (hil/o) where renal artery, vein, ureter, lymphatics, and nerves enter/exit the kidney. The “gateway” of the kidney.
KidneysTwo bean-shaped organs (nephr/o = Greek; ren/o = Latin) retroperitoneally at T12-L3. Right kidney slightly lower. ~1 million nephrons each. Only PARENCHYMAL tissue of the urinary system.
MicturitionThe act of urinating. Synonyms: urination, voiding. All three terms used interchangeably in clinical practice. ~5 times/day, ~1.5 L total.
NephronFunctional unit of the kidney — ~1 million per kidney. Glomerulus (glomerul/o) → Bowman’s capsule → proximal convoluted tubule → loop of Henle → distal convoluted tubule → collecting duct → renal pelvis.
Renal pelvisFunnel-shaped collecting area (pyel/o) inside each kidney. Collects urine from calices → channels to ureter. Pyelonephritis = infection of renal pelvis AND kidney — most common kidney infection.
TrigoneTriangular region (trigon/o) on inner bladder floor formed by two ureteral openings + urethral opening. Most common site of bladder infections and bladder cancer.
UretersTwo muscular tubes (ureter/o) from each kidney to the bladder via peristalsis. ~25-30 cm. ⚠ ureter/o (two tubes, kidney→bladder) ≠ urethr/o (one tube, bladder→outside).
UrethraSingle tube (urethr/o) from bladder to outside. Female ~4 cm (short → higher UTI risk). Male ~20 cm (through prostate + penis). External opening = urinary meatus (meat/o).
Urinary meatusExternal opening (meat/o) of the urethra. Pronunciation: mee-AY-tus. Meatotomy = incision to widen a stenotic opening.
UrinationProcess of releasing urine. Synonym for micturition and voiding. Controlled by detrusor muscle + internal (involuntary) and external (voluntary) urethral sphincters.
VoidingAnother term for urination/micturition. VCUG = voiding cystourethrography = imaging of bladder and urethra DURING urination (voiding).
Detailed anatomy of the kidney, nephron, urinary tract, and the critical ureter vs urethra distinction.
Renal tissue classification
Parenchymal tissue
Kidneys — the workers
Only the KIDNEYS (nephr/o, ren/o) do the actual work of the urinary system. Cortex (cortic/o) + medulla (medull/o) = the parenchyma that filters blood and produces urine.
Stromal tissue
Supporting structures
Supportive tissue helping kidneys function. Stromal structures: ureters (ureter/o), bladder (cyst/o, vesic/o), trigone (trigon/o), urethra (urethr/o), urinary meatus (meat/o).
Kidney gross structure
Cortex (cortic/o)
Outer layer
Contains glomeruli and proximal/distal convoluted tubules. Site of most filtration. Reddish-brown, granular appearance.
Medulla (medull/o)
Inner layer
Contains loops of Henle and collecting ducts. Creates osmotic gradient to concentrate urine. Organized as renal pyramids.
Renal pelvis (pyel/o)
Funnel-shaped collector
Collects urine from calices → channels to ureter. Pyelonephritis = infection here. Hydronephrosis = dilation from obstruction.
Hilum (hil/o)
Medial gateway
Entry/exit point: renal artery, vein, ureter, lymphatics, nerves. Concave medial indentation of the kidney.
Nephron — functional unit (~1 million per kidney)
ComponentFunction & clinical significance
Glomerulus (glomerul/o)Ball of capillaries where filtration begins. Pressure forces water and small solutes through. Proteins and RBCs normally blocked — if they appear in urine = glomerular damage (proteinuria, hematuria).
Bowman’s capsuleCup surrounding glomerulus. Collects filtrate → channels into proximal tubule. Together with the glomerulus = renal corpuscle.
Proximal convoluted tubuleReabsorbs ~67% of filtered Na, water, glucose, amino acids. Glucose completely reabsorbed unless blood glucose exceeds renal threshold (~180 mg/dL) → glycosuria = sign of diabetes mellitus.
Loop of Henle (nephronic loop)Creates osmotic gradient in the medulla for urine concentration. Without this gradient, massive water loss in dilute urine would occur.
Distal convoluted tubuleFine-tunes pH and electrolytes. Regulated by aldosterone (retains Na, excretes K) and ADH (antidiuretic hormone — water reabsorption).
Collecting duct → Renal pelvisADH controls final water reabsorption here. WITHOUT ADH (diabetes insipidus) → water not reabsorbed → massive dilute polyuria. Urine: collecting duct → calices → renal pelvis (pyel/o) → ureter.
⚠ Critical distinction: ureter vs urethra
Ureter (ureter/o) — TWO tubes
Two muscular tubes. Carry urine FROM each kidney TO the bladder via peristalsis. ~25-30 cm long. One ureter per kidney. Pathology: ureteritis, ureterocele, vesicoureteral reflux.
Memory: urETEr = “E” for Each kidney has one.
Urethra (urethr/o) — ONE tube
One tube from bladder to outside. Female ~4 cm (short → high UTI risk). Male ~20 cm (through prostate + penis). Pathology: urethritis, urethral stenosis, urethrolysis.
Memory: urETHra = “H” for only one, leads Home/outside.
Female vs male urethra
Female urethra (~4 cm): short distance for bacteria to travel from meatus to bladder → much higher UTI rate in women than men. Male urethra (~20 cm): long natural barrier against ascending infection. BPH can obstruct male urethra → urinary retention.

⚠ Critical suffix pairs
-scope vs -scopy
CystoSCOPE = the INSTRUMENT (the physical fiberoptic camera). CystoSCOPY = the PROCEDURE (the examination). "The urologist performed a cystoscopy using a cystoscope." -scope is a noun (thing); -scopy is a process (event).
-tripsy vs -lithotomy
lithoTRIPSY = CRUSHING a stone (ESWL — sound waves from outside, no incision). nephroLITHOTOMY = INCISION to REMOVE a stone (percutaneous surgical access). One crushes; one cuts.
-ptosis vs -pexy
NephroPTOSIS = kidney DROOPS downward (-ptosis = drooping). NephroPEXY = kidney FIXED surgically (-pexy = fixation). -ptosis is the PROBLEM; -pexy is the SOLUTION.
-uria vs -dipsia
poly-URIA = excessive URINE production (>3 L/day). poly-DIPSIA = excessive THIRST. Both appear together in DM and DI, but they describe different things (urine vs thirst).

Clinical terms organized by category. Almost every urinary symptom ends in -uria (condition of urine) — the most productive suffix in this chapter.
Urinary signs (objective, measurable)
Albuminuria
albumin/o + -uria
Protein (albumin) in urine. Sign of glomerular damage. Early marker of diabetic nephropathy. Normally absent from urine.
Glycosuria
glycos/o + -uria
Glucose in urine. Sign of diabetes mellitus. Normally absent. Appears when blood glucose >renal threshold (~180 mg/dL).
Hematuria
hemat/o + -uria
Blood in urine. Painless gross hematuria in older adult = bladder cancer until proven otherwise. Also: UTI, stones, trauma.
Pyuria
py/o + -uria
Pus (>5 WBCs/hpf) in urine. Sign of UTI. Causes cloudy/turbid urine appearance. Neither pus nor bacteria should normally appear in urine.
Azoturia
azot/o + -uria
Excess nitrogen waste (urea) in urine. Indicates impaired kidney function. Azotemia = nitrogenous waste in blood = uremia.
Bacteriuria
bacteri/o + -uria
Bacteria in urine (>100,000 CFU/mL on culture = significant). Indicates UTI. Urine is normally sterile.
Urinary symptoms (subjective)
Anuria
an- + ur/o + -ia
No urine output (<50 mL/day). MEDICAL EMERGENCY. Causes: severe AKI, bilateral obstruction, severe dehydration. Immediate intervention required.
Dysuria
dys- + ur/o + -ia
Painful or difficult urination — burning/stinging. #1 symptom of UTI. Also in urethral stenosis, STIs, kidney stones.
Oliguria
olig/o + -uria
Scanty urine output (50-400 mL/day). Sign of dehydration, early AKI, or obstruction. Less severe than anuria.
Polyuria
poly- + ur/o + -ia
Excessive urine production (>3 L/day). Causes: DM, DI (lack of ADH), diuretics, lithium toxicity, hypercalcemia.
Polydipsia
poly- + -dipsia
Excessive thirst. -dipsia = THIRST (not urine). Accompanies polyuria in DM and DI. Body loses fluid rapidly → thirst response.
Nocturia
noct/o + -uria
Excessive nighttime urination (>1 time/night). Causes: BPH, UTI, diabetes mellitus, congestive heart failure.
Incontinence
involuntary urine loss
Involuntary urine loss. Types: stress (coughing/sneezing), urge (sudden), overflow (retention → overflow), functional (mobility).
Urinary retention
inability to void
Inability to empty the bladder. Acute = painful emergency (catheterization). Causes: BPH (#1 in men), urethral stricture, anticholinergic medications.
Urgency
sudden urge to void
Sudden, compelling urge to urinate. Common in UTI, overactive bladder, urge incontinence.
Diabetes insipidus (DI) vs diabetes mellitus (DM)
Diabetes Insipidus (DI)
Cause: Lack of ADH or renal resistance to ADH
Mechanism: No ADH → collecting duct cannot reabsorb water → massive water loss
Symptoms: Polyuria (3-20 L/day), polydipsia
Urine: Very dilute, NO glucose
Blood glucose: Normal
Rx: Desmopressin (synthetic ADH)
Diabetes Mellitus (DM)
Cause: Insufficient insulin (Type 1) or insulin resistance (Type 2)
Mechanism: Hyperglycemia → exceeds renal threshold → glycosuria → osmotic diuresis
Symptoms: Polyuria, polydipsia, glycosuria, ketoacidosis (Type 1)
Urine: Glucose positive
Blood glucose: Elevated (hyperglycemia)
Rx: Insulin (Type 1); lifestyle + oral agents (Type 2)
Kidney & urinary tract disorders
Pyelonephritis
pyel/o + nephr/o + -itis
Bacterial infection of renal pelvis + kidney. Most common serious kidney infection. Ascending from bladder. Fever, flank pain, CVA tenderness, dysuria. Rx: fluoroquinolones.
Glomerulonephritis
glomerul/o + nephr/o + -itis
Glomerular inflammation → hematuria, proteinuria, hypertension, edema. Post-streptococcal or autoimmune. Can progress to renal failure.
Nephroptosis
nephr/o + -ptosis
Drooping/floating kidney — drops >2 vertebral levels when standing. Flank pain upright, relieved when lying down. Treated with nephropexy.
Hydronephrosis
hydr/o + nephr/o + -osis
Dilation of renal pelvis + calices from urinary obstruction (stone, tumor, BPH). Increased pressure → kidney damage. Bilateral → renal failure.
Nephrolithiasis
nephr/o + lith/o + -iasis
Kidney stones (80% calcium oxalate). Renal colic = severe flank pain radiating to groin. CT = gold standard. ESWL (≤2 cm) or PCNL (>2 cm).
CKD / Renal failure
chronic kidney disease
Progressive irreversible kidney function loss. Staged by GFR (1-5). Stage 5 = ESRD (<15 mL/min) → dialysis or transplant. Causes: diabetes (#1), hypertension (#2).
Cystitis
cyst/o + -itis
Bladder inflammation — usually E. coli. Classic UTI: dysuria, urgency, frequency. Most common in women. Rx: TMP-SMX or nitrofurantoin.
Cystocele
cyst/o + -cele
Herniation of bladder into vagina through weakened pelvic floor. Symptoms: pelvic pressure, incontinence. Tx: Kegel exercises, pessary, surgery.
Vesicoureteral reflux
vesic/o + ureter/o + re- + -flux
Abnormal backflow of urine from bladder BACK into ureters. Risk of ascending pyelonephritis. Common in children. Graded I-V.
Urethral stenosis
urethr/o + stenosis
Narrowing/stricture of urethra from scarring (STIs, trauma, catheterization). Weak urine stream, straining, retention.

Urinalysis (UA) — three components
Physical examination
Appearance, color, SG
Color: pale yellow (normal) → amber (concentrated) → red/brown (hematuria) → cloudy (pyuria). Specific gravity (SG): 1.005-1.030. DI = very low SG. Normal quantity: ~1.5-2 L/day.
Chemical (dipstick)
Glucose, protein, blood, pH
Glucose (DM), protein/albumin (glomerular damage), blood (hematuria), pH, nitrites (bacteria), leukocyte esterase (WBCs/pyuria), ketones (DKA), bilirubin (liver disease).
Microscopic examination
WBCs, RBCs, bacteria, casts
>5 WBCs/hpf = pyuria = infection. >3 RBCs/hpf = hematuria. Bacteria (normally absent). RBC casts = glomerulonephritis. Granular casts = AKI.
Laboratory tests
TestDefinition & clinical notes
BUN (blood urea nitrogen)Measures nitrogen-containing waste (urea) in blood. Normal: 7-20 mg/dL. Elevated BUN = azotemia. Causes: reduced renal function (CKD, AKI), dehydration, or high protein intake.
Creatinine clearanceMeasures how well kidneys filter creatinine. Creatinine is produced at a constant rate from muscle metabolism — ideal kidney function marker. Low clearance = poor kidney function.
GFR (glomerular filtration rate)Gold standard kidney function measure. Normal: >90 mL/min. Used to stage CKD: Stage 1 (>90) through Stage 5 ESRD (<15 → dialysis or transplant required).
Diagnostic & therapeutic procedures
ProcedureDefinition & notes
Cystoscopycyst/o + -scopy = visual examination of bladder interior using a cystoscope (fiberoptic camera via urethra). Diagnoses: hematuria source, tumors, stones. Also therapeutic: biopsy, stone removal, stent placement. Cystoscope = the instrument; cystoscopy = the procedure.
VCUGVoiding cystourethrography = X-ray of bladder + urethra DURING urination with contrast dye. Primary use: diagnosing vesicoureteral reflux (VUR) in children and urethral anatomy.
KUB (X-ray)Kidneys, Ureters, Bladder plain abdominal X-ray. Screens for radio-opaque kidney stones (calcium), bladder distension. Does NOT show uric acid stones (need CT for those).
CT scanNon-contrast CT = gold standard for kidney stones (detects ALL types). CT urogram (with contrast) = gold standard for urinary tract evaluation. Excellent anatomical detail.
Lithotripsy (ESWL)lith/o + -tripsy = CRUSHING a stone. Extracorporeal shock wave lithotripsy = sound waves focused from outside body → stone shatters → fragments pass in urine. Non-invasive. For stones ≤2 cm. ⚠ -tripsy (crush) ≠ -lithotomy (incision to remove).
Nephrolithotomy (PCNL)nephr/o + lith/o + -tomy = INCISION into kidney to remove stone. Percutaneous (small flank incision → nephroscope). For stones >2 cm or failed ESWL. More invasive than lithotripsy.
Nephropexynephr/o + -pexy = surgical FIXATION of drooping kidney (nephroptosis). Kidney sutured to surrounding tissue to prevent downward migration when standing. -pexy fixes the -ptosis.
Meatotomymeat/o + -tomy = surgical INCISION of the urinary meatus to widen it. For meatal stenosis (narrowed opening). Common in boys after circumcision complications.
CatheterFlexible tube through urethra into bladder to drain urine. Types: Foley (indwelling balloon), intermittent straight (temporary, lower infection risk), suprapubic (through abdominal wall). For: retention, ICU monitoring, post-surgical.
Nephrectomynephr/o + -ectomy = REMOVAL of a kidney. Radical (kidney + adrenal + lymph nodes for cancer), partial (preserve kidney tissue), donor (for transplant).
Dialysis — kidney replacement therapy
Hemodialysis
Blood filtered through machine
Blood removed → run through dialyzer (artificial kidney) → waste/fluid removed → blood returned. 3-4 hrs/session, 3 times/week. Requires AV fistula or graft for vascular access.
Peritoneal dialysis
Peritoneum as filter
Dialysate infused into peritoneal cavity → peritoneum acts as semipermeable membrane → waste diffuses out → fluid drained. CAPD (4x/day by patient) or CCPD (at night by machine).
Renal transplant
Best ESRD treatment
Donor kidney (living or cadaveric) implanted in the pelvis → connected to iliac vessels + bladder. Best long-term outcome for ESRD — superior to dialysis for quality of life and survival.
Pharmacology
Diuretics
Increase urine production → reduce fluid volume and BP. Loop (furosemide/Lasix — most potent), thiazide (HCTZ), potassium-sparing (spironolactone). For hypertension, edema, CHF.
Antidiuretics
Decrease urine production. Desmopressin (DDAVP = synthetic ADH) for diabetes insipidus and nocturnal enuresis. Reduces urine volume dramatically.
Antiinfectives
Antibiotics for urinary infections. First-line UTI: TMP-SMX, nitrofurantoin. Pyelonephritis: fluoroquinolones (ciprofloxacin). Always obtain urine culture when possible.
Anticholinergics
Reduce bladder spasms and overactive bladder. Oxybutynin, tolterodine. Block muscarinic receptors → relax detrusor muscle → reduce urgency and urinary frequency.
